# JLPT N5/N4 lower-listening audit evidence

This document is an evidence scaffold for an independent audit of Kakehashi's lower-level listening seeds. It establishes the official form and level boundaries; it does **not** judge any seed's Japanese, answer key, distractors, or assigned level.

## Evidence discipline

- **Official contract** means a current JLPT item-composition page, item-purpose sheet, level description, or FAQ statement.
- **Released-workbook observation** means a feature visible in the official 2018 workbook booklet, paired listening script, or audio. It is strong evidence of an authentic administered form, but not a promise that every future administration has identical counts, pauses, surface layouts, or content distributions.
- The 2018 workbook contains questions selected from post-2010 administrations and almost the number used in a real test. The JLPT separately warns that lightweight samples can differ from the actual booklet. Therefore this audit treats the workbook as form evidence, not a fixed current blueprint. ([official workbook index](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/samples/sampleindex.html), [official sample caveat](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/samples/forlearners.html))

## Authoritative contract

### Family availability and purpose

All three families below are present at **both N5 and N4** in the current official composition matrix. They occur in this order within Listening: task-based comprehension, comprehension of key points, verbal expressions, then quick response. ([current composition matrix](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/testsections.html))

| Family | Official purpose at N5 and N4 | Audit consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Task-based comprehension (課題理解) | Understand coherent spoken material, extract information needed to resolve a concrete issue, and identify the appropriate action. ([N5 purpose sheet](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/pdf/n5_e_revised.pdf), [N4 purpose sheet](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/pdf/n4_e_revised.pdf)) | The item needs a practical goal or decision. A generic detail-recall question is insufficient by itself. |
| Comprehension of key points (ポイント理解) | Understand coherent spoken material and narrow down relevant points using information given in advance. ([N5 purpose sheet](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/pdf/n5_e_revised.pdf), [N4 purpose sheet](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/pdf/n4_e_revised.pdf)) | The target information must be known before the body, so the listener can listen selectively. |
| Quick response (応答問題 / Quick response) | Hear a short utterance such as a question and choose an appropriate response. ([N5 purpose sheet](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/pdf/n5_e_revised.pdf), [N4 purpose sheet](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/pdf/n4_e_revised.pdf)) | Test interactional appropriateness, not merely whether a reply is grammatical or shares vocabulary with the prompt. |

The official purpose wording is the same for these families at N5 and N4. Level is therefore not established by the family label alone; the language, scene, length, inference load, and delivery must remain inside the separate level boundary below.

### N5 versus N4 boundary

| Dimension | N5 | N4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overall target | Some understanding of basic Japanese. N4 and N5 primarily measure basic Japanese learned in class. | Understanding of basic Japanese. N4 and N5 primarily measure basic Japanese learned in class. |
| Listening setting | Short conversations in very frequently encountered settings, explicitly including classrooms and one's immediate surroundings. | Conversations encountered in daily life; the official description does not impose N5's explicit classroom/short-conversation restriction. |
| Listening outcome | Pick out necessary information. | Generally follow the conversation's content. |
| Speed | Slow. The Japanese official wording is `ゆっくり`. | Somewhat slow. The Japanese official wording is `ややゆっくり`. |

Sources: [official English level summary](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/levelsummary.html) and [official Japanese level summary](https://www.jlpt.jp/about/levelsummary.html). The Japanese wording preserves the N5/N4 speed distinction more explicitly than the English translation.

The official sources publish **no numeric speech-rate target**, utterance-duration ceiling, vocabulary list, or grammar list for either level. Do not present an invented words-per-minute threshold as JLPT policy. The JLPT explains that it no longer publishes post-2010 content-specification lists because the test evaluates communicative use of language knowledge, not isolated lists. ([official FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/))

## Response and playback mechanics

The JLPT is computer-scored multiple choice. The general rule is four choices, with some Listening items using three. ([official FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/)) The exact mapping for these three lower-level families is visible in both official 2018 booklets and their paired scripts:

| Family | 2018 N5/N4 item sequence | Response presentation | Number of choices |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Task-based comprehension, Question 1 | Hear scene/speaker framing and the target question; hear the coherent body once; hear the target question again; choose one best answer. | Alternatives are visible in the booklet and may be text, illustrations, or structured combinations. They are not read aloud as answer choices. | Four printed alternatives, numbered 1-4. |
| Comprehension of key points, Question 2 | Hear scene/speaker framing and the target question; hear the coherent body once; hear the target question again; choose one best answer. | Alternatives are visible in the booklet and may be text or illustrations. They are not read aloud as answer choices. | Four printed alternatives, numbered 1-4. |
| Quick response, Question 4 | Hear one short prompt; then hear three candidate replies; choose the most appropriate reply. | The booklet explicitly says there are no pictures or similar response materials and supplies only a memo area; the reply text is audio-only. | Three spoken alternatives, numbered 1-3. |

Primary workbook evidence: [N5 booklet](https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2018/pdf/N5L.pdf), [N5 script](https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2018/pdf/N5script.pdf), [N4 booklet](https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2018/pdf/N4L.pdf), and [N4 script](https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2018/pdf/N4script.pdf). The paired official recordings are [N5 Q1](https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2018/mp3/N5Q1.mp3), [N5 Q2](https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2018/mp3/N5Q2.mp3), [N5 Q4](https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2018/mp3/N5Q4.mp3), [N4 Q1](https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2018/mp3/N4Q1.mp3), [N4 Q2](https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2018/mp3/N4Q2.mp3), and [N4 Q4](https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2018/mp3/N4Q4.mp3).

### Important playback conclusion

In the released 2018 Q1/Q2 materials, the **conversation or monologue body is delivered once**, not replayed wholesale. What occurs twice is the **target question**: once before the body to establish the listening goal and once after it before answering. Quick-response items present the prompt once and each of the three reply candidates once. This is an observation of the paired official workbook/script/audio, not a separately stated universal replay policy.

For a real-test-style Kakehashi mode, one body presentation is the evidence-backed behavior. Allowing manual replay can still be a useful practice-mode accommodation, but it should not be described as reproducing official exam conditions.

### N4/N5 instruction difference observed in 2018

The N4 Question 2 booklet explicitly announces a short interval after the initial question and before the body; the N5 Question 2 booklet does not state that interval in its printed instruction. ([N4 booklet, Question 2](https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2018/pdf/N4L.pdf), [N5 booklet, Question 2](https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2018/pdf/N5L.pdf)) This is a released-workbook observation, not enough evidence to define a permanent level-specific number of pause seconds.

## What the public material does not prove

1. It does not establish an exact numeric speech rate, pause duration, waveform duration, or maximum number of turns for either level.
2. It does not prove that every item in every administration uses the same mix of text answers, pictures, or combination diagrams within Q1/Q2.
3. It does not make 2018 item counts immutable current quotas. The workbook index says the volumes contain *almost* an actual test's number and are selections from prior administrations. ([official workbook index](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/samples/sampleindex.html))
4. It does not authorize copying or close adaptation. The official site states that the organizers own test-question copyright and specifically warns that listening recordings can contain third-party works. ([official FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/), [official workbook copyright notice](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/samples/sampleindex.html))
5. It does not prove level merely from a familiar topic. N5 versus N4 must be judged from the whole interaction: setting familiarity, length, linguistic load, amount of tracking, inference burden, and required outcome.
6. It does not prove that a grammatically possible response is a valid distractor. The booklet instruction requires one *best* answer, so context and conversational fit must leave a unique choice.

## Mechanics checklist for the independent seed audit

- [ ] Family is available at the assigned level.
- [ ] Task-based item has a concrete issue/goal and asks for the resulting action, choice, sequence, or necessary information.
- [ ] Key-point item exposes the listening target before the body and tests selective extraction rather than an unforeseen global-summary task.
- [ ] Quick-response item is a short prompt plus three **spoken** replies, with no printed reply text in real-test mode.
- [ ] Task-based/key-point item uses four **visible** alternatives and one clearly best answer.
- [ ] Real-test-style playback uses one body pass; Q1/Q2 may repeat the target question around that single pass rather than replaying the body.
- [ ] N5 scene is highly familiar and the exchange is short, slow, and focused on necessary information.
- [ ] N4 stays in daily life, may require following more of the exchange, and remains somewhat slow rather than near-natural.
- [ ] No numeric speed or duration claim is labeled official unless a new first-party specification is found.
- [ ] Wording and audio are original, not copied or closely adapted from released JLPT material.

## Content audit

### Scope and limits

An independent AI pass reviewed all 52 semantic seeds in
`web/src/features/jlpt/questions/generated/lower-listening-seeds.ts`: eight
task, eight key-point, and ten quick-response seeds at each of N5 and N4. The
review reconstructed every keyed answer against every distractor, checked the
Japanese and level boundary, compared presentation metadata to the contract
above, and compared conspicuous wording against the official 2018 N5/N4
scripts. Automated tests are reported separately because they do not establish
naturalness, level, originality, or a unique pragmatic answer.

This is **not native-Japanese or human JLPT editorial approval**. No seed may be
marked `human-approved` from this audit.

### Defects repaired in this audit

- **P1 originality - `N5-quick-more-tea-refusal`.** Its prompt was effectively
  the released N4 2018 quick-response prompt offering another cup of tea
  ([official N4 script](https://www.jlpt.jp/samples/sample2018/pdf/N4script.pdf)).
  The whole semantic item was replaced by the independently framed
  `N5-quick-dessert-choice`; this was not a vocabulary swap inside the released
  scenario.
- **P1 unique-answer risks - `N4-quick-copies-not-yet`,
  `N4-quick-reschedule-reservation`, and
  `N4-quick-submission-deadline`.** The former alternatives `はい、三枚でした`,
  `来週は水曜日が休みです`, and `はい、昨日出しました` could each function as
  a defensible response to the prompt. They were replaced with grammatical,
  topically plausible utterances that do not answer the requested completion,
  rescheduling, or deadline decision.
- **P1 unique-answer risk - `N5-quick-library-invitation`.** `もう図書館へ行きました`
  could work as a reason for declining the invitation. It was replaced so only
  the keyed acceptance actually responds to the invitation.
- **P2 naturalness - `N5-task-station-ticket-before-platform`.** The interview-like
  `電車に乗る前に、何をしますか` was replaced by the natural situational prompt
  `もう電車に乗れますか`; the same action chain and key remain.
- **P2 phone-number integrity - `N5-quick-phone-number-request`.** The purported
  mobile number stopped after four subscriber digits. It now supplies a
  complete `090-XXXX-XXXX` number and the semantic focus/explanation match it.
- **P2 explanation accuracy - `N5-quick-return-time`,
  `N5-quick-station-directions`, and `N4-quick-thanks-for-umbrella`.** These
  explanations respectively mislabeled a future activity as completed,
  quoted answer wording not present in the option, and described location and
  weather replies as lending requests. The explanations now describe the
  actual displayed candidates.

ID-specific regression assertions preserve every repair and exclude the
released tea-offer wording.

### Unresolved findings and release blockers

#### P1 - 200 generated records are not 200 distinct questions

The source has only **8/8/10 semantic items per level** for task/key/quick
response. The generation test nevertheless requires 200 records per family
while explicitly expecting only 8, 8, or 10 distinct `semanticKey` values.
`authoredListeningContent` applies no textual variant to these authored seeds;
the repeated records preserve the same question, script, choices, and
explanation while `makeOptions` merely rotates option order. Thus the six
nominal 200-record banks contain only 52 distinct lower-listening scenarios in
total, not 1,200. Calling these repeats `controlled-variant` or implying that a
learner can take many nonrepeating quizzes is not supported by the current
implementation.

This directly blocks the requested minimum of 200 original questions per
level and family. It must be addressed by adding independently authored and
reviewed semantic items, not by multiplying IDs or answer orders.

#### P2 - every task-based seed needs stronger distractors and more varied work

The N5 problem is systematic, not isolated. Examples include:

- `N5-task-supermarket-shopping-list`: only milk and bread participate in the
  dialogue; egg and water are free eliminations.
- `N5-task-classroom-teacher-notebook`: putting the notebook in a bag and
  carrying the teacher's chair have no support in the scene.
- `N5-task-library-return-before-borrowing`: going home and buying a bag are
  not credible competing actions.
- `N5-task-park-cleanup-trash-first`, `N5-task-clinic-write-name-first`, and
  `N5-task-rainy-trip-pack-umbrella` likewise contain two options that are not
  part of the heard decision. The same defect is present in the remaining N5
  task seeds.

At N4, `N4-task-delivery-choose-new-time`,
`N4-task-community-class-form-first`, `N4-task-office-poster-manager-check`,
`N4-task-cooking-preheat-oven`, `N4-task-hotel-return-key-before-luggage`,
`N4-task-school-trip-select-photos`, and `N4-task-laundry-check-pockets` each
include at least one action that was neither proposed nor made plausible.
`N4-task-bicycle-call-repair-shop` is somewhat stronger, but its answer is
still directly announced and confirmed. All eight N4 items ask essentially
the same “what first?” operation, usually signaled by `まず` or `先に`.

The rewrite should introduce all four competing actions into the heard plan,
make each distractor correspond to a missed constraint, superseded plan,
wrong order, or conditional branch, and give N4 more tracking/inference than
N5. Do not solve the problem by adding arbitrary turns or harder nouns.

### Presentation, timing, and level checks

- The seed union correctly requires four printed alternatives and
  `before-stimulus` timing for task/key items, and three `audioOnlyOptions` with
  `prompt-only` timing for quick response.
- The renderer speaks the task/key question before and after one body pass,
  appends the three numbered quick-response candidates to the audio, and hides
  their Japanese text behind neutral `Choice 1-3` controls. This matches the
  released-workbook mechanics documented above.
- Mock mode permits one continuous playback. Practice mode permits two and
  labels that behavior as practice; it does not present the replay as official
  test behavior.
- N5 and N4 use product-calibrated rates `0.82` and `0.88`. Their ordering is
  consistent with official “slow” versus “somewhat slow” descriptions, but
  these numbers are not official JLPT speech-rate specifications.
- The key-point sets show credible level differentiation. The task sets do not:
  N4 language is modestly richer, but the decision pattern and explicitness
  remain nearly identical to N5.
- No other close wording match was established in the released N5/N4 2018
  scripts. That limited comparison is a red-flag screen, not proof of global
  originality against every official administration or third-party source.

### N5 verdict — pass within the audited scope

All eight N5 task-based seeds now pass the remediation criteria:

- Every alternative is audibly grounded. The three distractors in each item
  represent an already completed or available item, another person's role, a
  later step, a rejected action, or an unmet condition rather than an unrelated
  category error.
- Each question has one uniquely supported answer. The relevant exclusions and
  ordering cues are explicit enough for N5 while still requiring the listener
  to distinguish the current assignment or next action from the other heard
  possibilities.
- The dialogue remains short, concrete, and situated in highly familiar N5
  settings: shopping, class, the library, a station, a park, home, a clinic,
  and a group trip. The Japanese is natural at a basic register.
- The set now varies its work across role assignment, prerequisite, current
  action, changed priority, and sequence recognition instead of relying only on
  an announced `まず` or `先に` cue.

No remaining P1 or P2 defect was found in these eight items.

### N4 verdict — pass within the audited scope

All eight N4 task-based seeds now pass the remediation criteria:

- Every alternative is anchored in the recording. Distractors preserve a
  discussed but ruled-out collection method, a completed or later registration
  step, a conditional approval branch, premature transport or purchase, a
  different sequence position, or an incorrect combination of heard details.
- Each item has one best answer after applying all stated constraints. In
  particular, the delivery and poster items require rejecting unavailable or
  conditional branches; the bicycle item requires connecting the shop-number
  lookup to a call; and the hotel, photo, and laundry items require retaining
  paired details.
- The Japanese is natural for basic daily-life interaction and remains inside
  N4. Compared with N5, these items meaningfully increase tracking through
  conditional branches, changed plans, multi-step procedures, criteria-based
  selection, and paired destinations rather than merely adding harder nouns.
- The questions still resolve practical goals or determine the learner's next
  action, so the family remains task-based comprehension rather than drifting
  into generic detail recall.

No remaining P1 or P2 defect was found in these eight items.

### Remaining limitations and release status

The 72-item expansion itself has no unresolved P1/P2 content blocker in this
AI audit. The integrated count nevertheless remains far below 200 independent
questions per level and family: it has **20 task, 20 key-point, and 22
quick-response semantic items per level**. The 200-record families repeat those
semantic items with rendering/choice-order variation. The engine correctly
prefers unseen semantic keys before cycling, but a user cannot receive 200
semantically nonrepeating items from any lower-listening family.

Accordingly, product copy may state “200 rendered records” only if it also
preserves the semantic-count distinction. It must not promise 200 original or
nonrepeating listening questions per type and level.

This remains AI review, not native-Japanese approval, human JLPT editorial
approval, audio performance review, or psychometric validation. The official
sources do not publish numeric speech-rate or difficulty calibration targets;
human listening-specialist review remains the proper final gate.
