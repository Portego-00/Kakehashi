# N5 language-knowledge expansion research

Research date: 2026-08-30

This note records the first-party constraints used to author the bounded N5
vocabulary and grammar expansion. It does not reproduce official question
wording. All added prompts, answer sets, and explanations are original.

## Verified N5 boundaries

The organizers describe N5 as understanding some basic Japanese. Its reading
target is typical expressions and sentences written in hiragana, katakana, and
basic kanji. N4 and N5 chiefly measure basic Japanese learned in class. This
supports familiar classroom and everyday contexts, short clauses, concrete
referents, and basic written forms; it does not support importing N4 discourse
or inference demands merely to make an item harder.

The organizers intentionally publish no post-2010 vocabulary, kanji, or grammar
list. The FAQ directs item writers and learners to the competence summaries,
composition tables, and samples instead. Consequently, this tranche does not
claim that a word or form is on an official N5 syllabus. Level fit is a bounded
editorial judgment against the official N5 activity description and samples.

## Official constructs preserved

The N5 purpose sheet defines these relevant families:

- **Kanji reading:** identify the reading of a word written in kanji.
- **Orthography:** identify kanji or katakana for a word presented in hiragana.
- **Contextually defined expressions:** choose the word whose meaning/use fits
  the supplied sentence.
- **Paraphrases:** choose an expression with similar meaning.
- **Selecting grammar form:** choose the grammar form suitable for one sentence.
- **Sentence composition:** form one syntactically accurate, meaningful sentence
  and answer the numbered fragment at `★`.

The FAQ confirms computer-scored multiple choice, normally four choices. The
2018 N5 vocabulary and grammar booklets were reviewed for presentation and
distractor mechanics only: a focused written word in a short context; four
compact reading/writing alternatives; one contextual blank; four natural form
choices; and four fragments for `★` composition. No official stem, sentence,
answer set, or scenario was reused.

## Authoring decisions for this tranche

1. Use concrete daily-life and classroom domains: entrances, times of day,
   weather, names, telephone calls, schedules, countries, colors, simple skills,
   past years, meals, shopping, family gifts, and routine actions.
2. Keep one decisive cue in every context blank. A distractor may be a real
   basic word or form, but the complete sentence must rule it out.
3. Use visually or phonologically plausible reading/orthography distractors
   without accepting a second standard spelling.
4. Keep grammar rivals well formed where practical and use surrounding context
   to distinguish time, accompaniment, request, invitation, polarity, and
   tense.
5. For composition, prefer fixed dependencies such as adjective negation,
   `どこへも` plus a negative predicate, relative-clause attachment, and
   `ながら` clause pairing. Avoid relying only on flexible Japanese adjunct
   order.
6. Give every new context, grammar, and composition seed a stable semantic ID.
   Surface substitutions and option rotation remain variants, not new content.
7. Retain `machine-validated` status. This research and the automated tests are
   not proficient-human or native-speaker approval.

## Originality and validation limit

The official workbooks are copyright-protected released test material. They
were used to understand format and level texture, not as a source of sentences
to adapt. Automated checks can detect internal collisions and selected copied
phrase red flags, but they cannot prove corpus-wide originality or naturalness.
A proficient Japanese editor familiar with N5 item writing must still review
every new semantic item before any `human-approved` claim.
