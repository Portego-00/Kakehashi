# N3 language-knowledge expansion research

Research date: 2026-08-30

This note records the first-party constraints used for the bounded N3
language-knowledge expansion. It is an authoring audit, not a claim that a
proficient Japanese editor or native speaker approved the resulting items.

## Independent N3 boundary

The official summary calls N3 a bridge between the classroom-oriented N4/N5
group and the broader real-life N1/N2 group. Its target is understanding
Japanese used in everyday situations to a certain degree. N3 readers should
handle concrete everyday material, summary information such as headlines, and
the main point of somewhat difficult everyday writing when rephrasing support
is available. This supports concrete work, school, public-service, schedule,
application, and problem-solving contexts with moderate clause linking. It
does not justify N1-style abstraction or dense N2 commentary merely to make an
isolated vocabulary or grammar item harder.

The organizers do not publish a post-2010 vocabulary, kanji, or grammar list.
The FAQ instead directs authors and learners to the level summaries, item
composition tables, and samples. Therefore, calling an isolated word or form
“N3” remains a bounded editorial judgment; it is not certification against an
official syllabus.

## Official language-knowledge constructs preserved

The N3 purpose sheet specifies these relevant families:

- kanji reading: identify the reading of a word written in kanji;
- orthography: identify the kanji form of a word shown in hiragana;
- contextually defined expressions: select the word whose meaning is fixed by
  the sentence context;
- paraphrases: select a word or expression with similar meaning;
- usage: judge appropriate use of a word in a sentence;
- selecting grammar form: choose the form that suits a sentence;
- sentence composition: form a sentence that is both syntactically accurate
  and meaningful; and
- text grammar: judge fit within a larger text flow.

The FAQ confirms that most JLPT questions have four options. The released 2018
N3 vocabulary and grammar booklets were reviewed for mechanics only: compact
contextual readings and spellings, plausible lexical-category distractors,
usage alternatives, grammar selected within enough surrounding context, and
four fragments with a scored `★` position. No released sentence, scenario,
answer set, or distinctive wording was copied or adapted.

## Tranche authoring rules

1. Use everyday but adult contexts: deadlines, applications, meetings,
   transport, public facilities, health advice, scheduling, and resolving
   ordinary problems.
2. Give contextual and grammar items a decisive cue; a real Japanese word or
   form may distract, but it must not produce a second defensible reading.
3. Keep usage distractors sentence-shaped and related to the target's likely
   argument structure, while ensuring only one standard collocation/use.
4. Use moderate N3 dependencies in compositions, including `だけでなく`,
   continuing time with `てからずっと`, purpose `ように`, a considered decision with `ことにする`,
   discovery with `たところ`, and a concessive `ても` condition.
5. Give all 30 source concepts explicit stable source IDs. Runtime lexical and
   usage identities remain their stable target surface/focus; cloze and
   composition identities use the explicit semantic IDs directly.
6. Preserve `machine-validated` runtime status. Automated uniqueness,
   rendering, and mock-compatibility checks do not amount to human approval.

## Originality and validation limits

The official workbooks contain released copyrighted test items. They were used
only to compare construct and presentation. Internal collision and attribution
screens cannot prove corpus-wide originality, naturalness, level fit, or the
absence of every short conventional phrase found elsewhere. Before any
`human-approved` status, a proficient Japanese editor familiar with N3 item
writing must review each source item and its distractors.
